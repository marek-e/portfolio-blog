# DESIGN.md — Artistic Direction

## The vision

**A cinematic world for a curious builder.**

This portfolio should feel like stepping into a beautifully imagined world: a place where engineering, exploration, and creativity naturally belong together. It introduces a real developer and their work through an atmosphere that is welcoming, imaginative, and quietly sophisticated.

The experience is not a game, a corporate dashboard, or a typical developer portfolio. It is an **interactive editorial journey** with the warmth of an illustrated storybook and the refinement of a contemporary digital product.

**Core feeling:** curious, thoughtful, magical, human, and premium.

## The visual universe

Think of golden-hour valleys, distant mountains, wildflowers, clouds, gentle moonlight, and little discoveries along the way. The environments have cinematic scale but remain soft and inviting. They can shift between a sunlit, pastel world and a deep, luminous night, like two moods of the same place.

The imagery should combine **storybook illustration, sophisticated 3D volume, and painterly atmosphere**. Depth comes from lighting, layering, perspective, and material—not from adding effects everywhere.

Hand-drawn accents, delicate stars, trails, and annotations can make the world feel personal and lived-in. They should feel intentional and occasional, never like random decoration.

## Melo and Orbi

Melo and Orbi are the recognizable heart of this world. Preserve their established character design, silhouette, colors, expressive qualities, and relationship as shown in the reference artwork.

Melo represents curiosity, care, and the joy of building. Orbi adds a light touch of intelligence, companionship, and wonder. They may welcome visitors, accompany a transition, or quietly illuminate a moment of discovery.

They are **guides and companions**, not the subjects of every section. The portfolio must remain clearly about the developer, the projects, and the ideas behind them.

## The signature aesthetic: illustrated, dimensional, translucent

The portfolio blends **playful editorial design**, **hand-drawn pastel illustration**, **premium tactile 3D**, and **liquid glass / glassmorphism**. These aren't competing trends: each brings a different quality to the same visual world. Illustration makes it human; volume makes it tangible; glass makes it airy; editorial composition makes it clear.

### Hand-drawn character and pastel vector illustration

Build from a friendly, imperfect drawing language: rounded geometry, expressive shapes, subtle asymmetry, sketch-like arrows, handwritten annotations, little stars, and exploratory lines. Pastel-colored vector artwork brings clarity and simplicity, while occasional grain, pencil marks, or paper texture keeps it from feeling sterile or overly digital.

The illustration style should feel **deliberately handmade but professionally composed**. Favor charming visual details with a reason to exist. Let playful geometry and textured marks coexist with precise typography and readable interface elements. Avoid turning every section into a doodle-filled canvas.

### Premium 3D for presence, depth, and volume

Use thoughtfully rendered 3D to give selected elements a feeling of physical presence. This is not limited to hero illustrations: **buttons, cards, featured project previews, category icons, badges, floating objects, and key calls to action** can all benefit from gentle volume when it reinforces their importance.

Think softly sculpted, touchable materials: matte ceramic, velvety resin, shaped paper, rounded glass, and subtle molded surfaces. Favor realistic but diffused light, soft contact shadows, restrained highlights, and layered edges. A button might feel slightly raised; a feature card might float above the page; a small icon might resemble a carefully crafted object.

**Depth should establish hierarchy, not become an effect applied uniformly.** Allow simple, flat components to sit beside dimensional highlights so the special moments genuinely stand out. Avoid overly glossy toys, bulky bevels, and exaggerated shadows that make the UI feel childish.

### Liquid glass meets glassmorphism

Use glass as a sophisticated intermediate layer between the illustrated environment and the content. Combine the recognizable softness of **frosted glassmorphism** with selective **liquid-glass qualities**: luminous edges, gentle refraction, subtle specular highlights, and a sense of translucency that responds to what's behind it.

This material suits floating navigation, compact overlays, contextual controls, highlighted cards, and selected interactive elements. It can make the interface feel suspended within Melo's world without hiding the world itself. Glass should look refined and atmospheric rather than like a generic transparent rectangle.

Use it sparingly. Large reading surfaces, detailed project descriptions, and code examples should remain calm and legible. Glass must never compromise contrast, focus visibility, or the clarity of an interaction. Avoid stacking translucent surfaces endlessly or combining heavy blur, distortion, and glare in the same place.

### How the materials work together

A memorable composition might pair a **flat pastel drawing** with a **small sculptural 3D detail**, then place a **subtle liquid-glass control** in the foreground. The three layers should have compatible lighting, palette, proportions, and visual weight.

The balance is intentional: **illustration supplies warmth, 3D supplies substance, glass supplies atmosphere, and editorial design supplies discipline.** Every page may emphasize a different material, but the overall universe should remain recognizable.

## Color, light, and atmosphere

The palette lives between warm pastel daylight and atmospheric midnight. Imagine cream, peach, lavender, dusty rose, moonlit blue, deep violet, and small touches of glowing gold.

Color should feel discovered through the environment, not applied as a set of unrelated bright accents. Light is an essential part of the identity: hazy sunlight, soft shadows, glowing windows, gentle reflections, and luminous details.

**Light mode and dark mode are two chapters of the same world.** The scene changes mood, but the character and visual identity remain consistent.

## Typography and composition

Combine the personality of editorial storytelling with the precision of a developer's workspace. Expressive headlines can feel warm and memorable; supporting text should be calm, direct, and easy to scan. Monospaced details can subtly reference code and engineering without dominating the entire experience.

Give illustrations space to breathe. Use clear composition, deliberate hierarchy, and moments of visual quiet. Not every section needs a large background scene or a mascot appearance.

The interface should feel **crafted, not crowded**.

## How the journey unfolds

The **homepage** makes a strong emotional first impression: an atmospheric setting, an unmistakable personal introduction, and a glimpse of Melo's world.

**Projects** bring the focus back to real products and decisions. Screenshots, outcomes, and explanations should be the heroes; the visual world frames them rather than obscuring them.

**Experience** feels like a journey with meaningful milestones. The timeline remains professional, legible, and grounded in actual achievements.

**The blog** feels like a refined developer's journal. Articles are generous, readable, and thoughtfully structured. Code, diagrams, notes, and technical examples take priority over decoration.

**Contact moments** should feel warm and inviting, like reaching the end of a trail and finding an open door.

## Interaction and motion

Motion should communicate life and depth: drifting clouds, faint fireflies, subtle parallax, gentle shifts in light, and restrained character reactions. Interactions should feel responsive and polished, never noisy or distracting.

The world should be just as compelling when motion is reduced or absent. Reading, navigation, and comprehension must always come before spectacle.

## The premium balance

Premium does **not** mean maximum gloss, blur, or animation. It comes from restraint: beautiful light, consistent illustration quality, confident spacing, refined materials, and care for small details.

Keep a productive tension between the whimsical world and the seriousness of the work. The result should be friendly without becoming childish, immersive without becoming a game, and sophisticated without becoming cold.

## Creative guardrails

**Lean into:** cinematic composition; poetic but purposeful storytelling; warm pastels and deep nocturnal tones; playful hand-drawn accents; pastel vector illustrations; selective premium 3D volume; refined liquid glass and frosted-glass layers; expressive, consistent mascots; technical clarity; moments of surprise.

**Avoid:** generic SaaS gradients; glass on every surface; overdone refraction or blur; excessive neon or glow; plastic-looking toy interfaces; 3D depth on every element; random decorative doodles; overcrowded layouts; inconsistent mascot renderings; 3D assets with mismatched lighting; animation for its own sake; scenic artwork behind long-form text.

## The north star

> **Build a world people want to explore, while making the developer's work unmistakably clear.**

Every new page, illustration, or interaction should strengthen that balance: **wonder in the atmosphere, clarity in the content, and care in the details.**
